1. Purpose

This document establishes the canonical governance framework for identifying, documenting, verifying, preserving, monitoring, and auditing third-party rights associated with the QSSI™ / QVP™ Global System V11.

Its objectives are to ensure that third-party materials are:

- accurately identified;
- provenance-traceable;
- supported by verifiable evidence where reasonably practicable;
- governed according to their applicable licenses, contractual terms, and legal rights;
- clearly separated from original QSSI™ / QVP™ intellectual property;
- protected from accidental relicensing;
- appropriately attributed;
- transparently documented;
- consistently governed across releases; and
- independently auditable.

This register serves as a governance, provenance, attribution, dependency, and compliance record.

It does not replace:

- applicable law;
- original third-party licenses;
- contractual agreements;
- provider terms of use;
- database-right protections;
- institutional requirements; or
- professional legal advice.

---

2. Fundamental Rights Principle

Third-party materials remain governed by their original rights framework.

Neither inclusion within, nor distribution alongside, a QSSI™ / QVP™ artifact changes the applicable legal rights of third-party material.

Accordingly:

- repository publication does not transfer ownership;
- archival publication does not transfer ownership;
- DOI assignment does not transfer ownership;
- ZIP packaging does not transfer ownership;
- reproducibility packaging does not transfer ownership;
- repository-level licensing does not override component-level licensing;
- original rights remain independently applicable.

Only a lawful rights holder may grant or authorize rights over the relevant material.

---

3. Rights Separation Principle

The QSSI™ / QVP™ Global System adopts strict rights separation.

The following categories remain legally independent:

- Original QSSI™ / QVP™ Intellectual Property
- Public Research Material expressly designated by the project
- Third-Party Intellectual Property
- Public Domain Material
- Mixed-Rights Artifacts

No category shall automatically inherit the legal status of another.

---

4. No Automatic Relicensing

The inclusion of third-party material within:

- repositories;
- publications;
- datasets;
- documentation;
- software packages;
- computational workflows;
- notebooks;
- reproducibility archives;
- GitHub repositories;
- Zenodo records;
- DOI publications; or
- release packages

does not automatically subject that material to:

- CC BY-NC-ND 4.0;
- any QSSI™ / QVP™ proprietary license;
- any repository-level license;
- any public research license; or
- any implied commercial authorization.

Every component remains governed according to its own applicable rights.

---

5. Rights Governance Hierarchy

Where multiple governance sources exist, the following order applies:

1. Applicable law
2. Mandatory statutory obligations
3. Binding contractual terms
4. Original third-party license
5. Independent intellectual-property rights
6. Database rights
7. Artifact-level rights declaration
8. Repository governance documentation
9. General explanatory documentation

Lower-level governance documents shall not override higher-level legal obligations.

Where uncertainty exists:

VERIFICATION_STATUS = REVIEW_REQUIRED

---

Purpose

The matrix records evidence observations produced by the QSSI™ / QVP™ Rights Verification Workflow.

Its function is to preserve rights-related evidence in a structured, machine-readable format.

It is not intended to determine ownership or grant legal permissions.

---

Covered Providers

The matrix currently includes evidence classifications for providers such as:

- International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- SIPRI
- World Bank Worldwide Governance Indicators (WGI)
- Fund for Peace (Fragile States Index)
- Energy Institute / World Energy

Future releases may expand this list.

---

Evidence Methodology

The matrix intentionally adopts a conservative methodology.

Where sufficient evidence was not established, the matrix records that absence rather than inferring legal conclusions.

Typical values include:

- NOT_ESTABLISHED
- NONE
- False
- REVIEW_REQUIRED

These values indicate only that the corresponding rights position was not established from the reviewed evidence.

They do not imply that permission exists or does not exist.

---

Non-Inference Rule

The Conservative Provider Rights Evidence Matrix shall not be interpreted as:

- a legal opinion;
- a legal determination;
- ownership confirmation;
- copyright confirmation;
- license validation;
- permission to reproduce;
- permission to redistribute;
- permission for commercial use;
- permission to create derivative works;
- sublicensing authority;
- authorization to provide third-party material through software services; or
- any transfer of rights.

Evidence classification is distinct from legal authorization.

10. Evidence Standard

Rights classifications should be supported by reliable evidence wherever reasonably practicable.

Acceptable evidence may include:

- official license texts;
- official provider documentation;
- official repository license files;
- official API terms;
- official dataset documentation;
- institutional documentation;
- provider rights statements;
- documented permissions; and
- other authoritative evidence.

Unsupported assumptions shall not replace evidence.

---

11. Verification Model

Recommended verification states include:

- VERIFIED
- CONDITIONALLY_VERIFIED
- REVIEW_REQUIRED
- UNKNOWN
- CONFLICT_DETECTED
- EXPIRED_OR_CHANGED
- WITHDRAWN
- NOT_APPLICABLE

A VERIFIED status reflects review against available evidence only.

It is not a comprehensive legal determination.

12. Software Dependency Governance

Software dependencies shall be governed independently from original QSSI™ / QVP™ software.

Each significant dependency should, where reasonably practicable, record:

- package identity;
- ecosystem;
- version;
- upstream provider;
- license;
- dependency scope;
- direct/transitive status;
- notice obligations;
- security review status;
- integrity references; and
- verification status.

14. Provenance

Every significant third-party component should remain traceable.

Recommended provenance includes:

- provider;
- source;
- persistent identifier;
- DOI (where available);
- version;
- retrieval date;
- transformation history;
- harmonization;
- normalization;
- derivation; and
- processing references.

---

15. Mixed Rights

A single artifact may contain multiple independent rights regimes.

Mixed-rights artifacts should preserve the identity of every applicable rights framework.

Repository-level licensing shall not override component-level rights.

19. Prohibited Assumptions

The following assumptions are expressly rejected:

- Online availability equals unrestricted use.
- GitHub automatically means open source.
- DOI automatically transfers ownership.
- Public download automatically permits redistribution.
- Research automatically permits commercial use.
- Repository licensing overrides component licensing.
- Transformation removes underlying rights.
- Reproducibility creates redistribution rights.
- Repository inclusion creates ownership.
- Absence of notice establishes public-domain status.

Every material rights position requires evidence-based review.

---

20. Canonical Governance Declaration

The QSSI™ / QVP™ Global System recognizes and preserves the independent rights of third-party authors, licensors, software developers, institutions, data providers, database rights holders, and other rights holders.

Third-party materials remain governed by their applicable licenses, contractual terms, provenance conditions, database rights, intellectual-property rights, data-use restrictions, and applicable law.

No third-party component is automatically relicensed, transferred, converted into proprietary material, or converted into public research material merely because it appears within, accompanies, or is distributed alongside a QSSI™ / QVP™ artifact.

Where rights remain uncertain, the component shall be conservatively classified as REVIEW_REQUIRED until sufficient evidence supports a more specific determination.
