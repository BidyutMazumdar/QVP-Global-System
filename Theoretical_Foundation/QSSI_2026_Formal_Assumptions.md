## Purpose

The Quantum Sovereign Security Index (QSSI) is a multidimensional sovereign benchmarking framework designed to evaluate national preparedness, governance capacity, technological resilience, cybersecurity readiness, institutional robustness, and economic resilience under conditions of accelerating digital transformation, artificial intelligence deployment, cyber risk escalation, and post-quantum technological transition.

The framework provides a reproducible and transparent methodology for comparative sovereign assessment across countries using a unified composite architecture.

---

## Formal Assumption A1 — Sovereign Comparability

All sovereign entities included within the framework are assumed to be comparable analytical units after normalization and methodological harmonization.

Cross-country comparisons are interpreted as relative benchmark assessments rather than absolute measures of national capability.

---

## Formal Assumption A2 — Indicator Validity

Each source indicator is assumed to capture a meaningful component of sovereign preparedness within its respective dimension.

The framework assumes that source datasets maintain sufficient methodological quality, international comparability, and institutional credibility for comparative analysis.

---

## Formal Assumption A3 — Multidimensionality

Sovereign security is assumed to be inherently multidimensional.

No single dimension is assumed to fully explain national readiness.

The framework therefore integrates four complementary dimensions:

| Dimension | Description |
|------------|------------|
| AI | Artificial Intelligence Governance and Readiness |
| LEGAL | Rule of Law and Regulatory Quality |
| PQC | Post-Quantum Cybersecurity Readiness |
| RES | Economic and Financial Resilience |

---

## Formal Assumption A4 — Non-Dominance Principle

No individual dimension is assumed to possess complete explanatory authority over sovereign preparedness.

Composite assessment requires simultaneous consideration of all framework dimensions.

---

## Formal Assumption A5 — Information Preservation

Normalization procedures are assumed to preserve meaningful relative ordering among countries while enabling cross-dimensional comparability.

Transformation procedures are not assumed to create new information.

---

## Formal Assumption A6 — Missing Data Neutrality

Observed and imputed values are explicitly distinguished throughout the framework.

Imputation procedures are assumed to minimize systematic distortion while preserving analytical continuity.

Observed and imputed variables are maintained separately for transparency and auditability.

---

## Formal Assumption A7 — Confidence Stratification

Data completeness influences confidence but does not automatically determine sovereign performance.

Confidence scores are interpreted as measures of evidentiary support rather than measures of national capability.

Confidence tiers are defined as:

| Tier | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|
| A | Highest Confidence |
| B | High Confidence |
| C | Moderate Confidence |
| D | Limited Observational Coverage |

---

## Formal Assumption A8 — Entropy Information Principle

Entropy weighting is assumed to capture informational diversity and discriminatory power within indicators.

Indicators exhibiting greater informational differentiation contribute proportionally more to composite weighting structures.

---

## Formal Assumption A9 — CRITIC Variability Principle

CRITIC weighting is assumed to capture informational importance through variance and inter-indicator conflict.

Indicators contributing greater unique information receive proportionally greater influence.

---

## Formal Assumption A10 — PCA Variance Principle

Principal Component Analysis is assumed to identify latent variance structures present within multidimensional datasets.

PCA weights reflect underlying statistical variance rather than normative policy priorities.

---

## Formal Assumption A11 — Weight Aggregation Stability

The synthesis of Entropy, CRITIC, and PCA weighting structures is assumed to reduce dependence upon any single weighting methodology.

The final weighting structure is intended to improve robustness through methodological diversification.

---

## Formal Assumption A12 — Composite Score Validity

Composite scores are assumed to provide a meaningful summary representation of multidimensional sovereign preparedness.

Composite scores are interpreted as benchmark measures rather than deterministic predictions.

---

## Formal Assumption A13 — Ranking Interpretation

Rankings represent relative positions within the evaluated country set.

Rank differences do not imply proportional performance differences.

Small score differences may correspond to larger rank differences and vice versa.

---

## Formal Assumption A14 — Tier Interpretation

Tier classifications are intended to facilitate interpretability and policy communication.

Tier membership should not be interpreted as categorical evidence of superiority or inferiority.

---

## Formal Assumption A15 — Robustness Principle

A valid composite framework should exhibit reasonable stability under controlled methodological perturbations.

Robustness assessments therefore evaluate:

- Dimension removal
- Weight perturbation
- Rank sensitivity
- Correlation preservation

---

## Formal Assumption A16 — Monte Carlo Stability Principle

Repeated simulation provides a probabilistic assessment of ranking persistence.

Countries exhibiting lower rank variance are interpreted as possessing greater ranking stability under uncertainty.

---

## Formal Assumption A17 — External Validation Principle

External benchmark comparison is assumed to provide evidence regarding convergent validity.

High correlation indicates structural similarity but does not imply conceptual identity.

External validation is interpreted as supporting evidence rather than definitive proof of validity.

---

## Formal Assumption A18 — Incremental Contribution Principle

Each framework dimension is assumed to contribute information beyond that provided by remaining dimensions.

Dimension-removal experiments evaluate incremental informational contribution.

---

## Formal Assumption A19 — Reproducibility Principle

Independent researchers should be capable of reproducing published results using the released artifacts, methodology, metadata, and computational workflow.

Reproducibility is treated as a foundational requirement of the framework.

---

## Formal Assumption A20 — FAIR+D Principle

The framework assumes that scientific utility is enhanced through adherence to FAIR+D principles:

- Findable
- Accessible
- Interoperable
- Reusable
- Deterministically Reproducible

---

## Formal Assumption A21 — Auditability Principle

All published outputs are assumed to be traceable to documented methodological procedures.

Traceability includes:

- Data lineage
- Metadata records
- Validation outputs
- Manifest records
- Artifact registries
- Hash registries

---

## Formal Assumption A22 — Temporal Scope

QSSI 2026 represents a benchmark assessment corresponding to the data environment available during the 2026 release cycle.

Results should be interpreted within the temporal context of the underlying source data.

---

## Formal Assumption A23 — Policy Neutrality

The framework is designed as an analytical benchmarking system.

Scores, rankings, and tiers are descriptive outputs and do not constitute policy prescriptions.

---

## Formal Assumption A24 — Institutional Use

The framework may support:

- Academic research
- Comparative governance analysis
- Policy benchmarking
- Sovereign risk assessment
- Strategic planning
- Institutional evaluation

Interpretation remains subject to methodological limitations and contextual analysis.

---

## Formal Assumption A25 — Scope Limitation

QSSI does not directly measure:

- Military capability
- Diplomatic influence
- Cultural power
- Electoral outcomes
- National ideology

The framework specifically evaluates dimensions represented within the published methodological architecture.

---

## Formal Assumption A26 — Deterministic Release Principle

Published outputs correspond to archived release artifacts.

All reported results are derived from the documented release package and associated validation records.
