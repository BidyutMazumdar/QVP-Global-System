Executive Summary

The FAIR+D Canon™ Governance Principles establish the normative, methodological, operational, and institutional foundations for transparent, reproducible, traceable, interoperable, and defensible computational systems.

These principles provide a governance architecture for data-intensive research, sovereign analytics, computational policy intelligence, institutional benchmarking, and evidence-based decision support systems.

The framework promotes analytical integrity, methodological accountability, computational transparency, and long-term reproducibility across the full lifecycle of data, models, algorithms, and published outputs.

---

1. Purpose

The purpose of these governance principles is to establish a common institutional framework for the responsible development, validation, deployment, maintenance, and preservation of computational analytical systems.

The principles support:

- Methodological rigor
- Data accountability
- Computational transparency
- Reproducible research
- Institutional trust
- Evidence-based decision support
- Long-term sustainability
- International interoperability

---

2. Governance Philosophy

FAIR+D Canon™ adopts a governance-first approach to computational systems.

Analytical outputs derive legitimacy not solely from technical sophistication but from transparent processes, documented evidence, reproducible methodologies, and accountable governance structures.

Governance therefore extends across:

- Data
- Metadata
- Algorithms
- Models
- Pipelines
- Documentation
- Validation
- Publication

---

3. Core Governance Principles

Principle 1 — Transparency

All significant methodological decisions shall be documented and publicly explainable.

Transparency applies to:

- Data sources
- Variable definitions
- Assumptions
- Transformations
- Models
- Validation procedures
- Outputs

Transparency strengthens interpretability and institutional trust.

---

Principle 2 — Traceability

Every analytical output shall maintain documented lineage to originating data assets and computational procedures.

Traceability requires:

- Provenance records
- Transformation histories
- Version references
- Processing documentation

Traceability enables complete reconstruction of analytical workflows.

---

Principle 3 — Reproducibility

Independent execution using equivalent inputs and procedures should generate equivalent analytical outcomes.

Reproducibility requires:

- Version control
- Metadata preservation
- Pipeline documentation
- Computational consistency
- Archival permanence

Reproducibility remains a foundational requirement of defensible analytics.

---

Principle 4 — Accountability

All analytical systems shall maintain identifiable responsibility structures governing development, validation, maintenance, and publication.

Accountability includes:

- Documentation ownership
- Validation oversight
- Change management
- Preservation responsibility

Accountability strengthens institutional reliability.

---

Principle 5 — Integrity

Analytical systems shall preserve the integrity of data, metadata, models, and outputs throughout their lifecycle.

Integrity includes:

- Data consistency
- Structural validity
- Computational reliability
- Preservation safeguards

Integrity protects analytical credibility.

---

Principle 6 — Explainability

Computational outputs shall be interpretable to relevant stakeholders.

Explainability requires:

- Methodological documentation
- Variable transparency
- Model interpretation
- Assumption disclosure

Explainability promotes informed decision-making.

---

Principle 7 — Defensibility

Published outputs shall be supported by documented evidence, transparent methodologies, and reproducible procedures.

Defensibility requires:

- Validation records
- Provenance documentation
- Methodological justification
- Computational auditability

Defensibility represents the defining extension of FAIR+D beyond conventional FAIR principles.

---

4. Data Governance Principles

Data governance provides the foundation for analytical reliability.

Source Authenticity

All incorporated datasets should originate from documented and verifiable sources.

Data Quality

Datasets should undergo validation procedures addressing:

- Completeness
- Consistency
- Accuracy
- Interoperability

Provenance Preservation

Data lineage shall remain documented throughout the analytical lifecycle.

Metadata Stewardship

Metadata shall accompany all analytical datasets.

---

5. Computational Governance Principles

Computational procedures shall remain transparent and reproducible.

Pipeline Documentation

Analytical pipelines shall maintain documented processing logic.

Version Management

All computational artifacts shall support version tracking.

Validation Requirements

Analytical outputs shall undergo documented validation procedures.

Auditability

Computational workflows should be reconstructable from preserved records.

---

6. Model Governance Principles

Where analytical models are employed, governance responsibilities include:

- Model documentation
- Assumption disclosure
- Performance evaluation
- Version control
- Validation reporting

Model governance promotes analytical accountability and methodological consistency.

---

7. Publication Governance Principles

Publication constitutes the final stage of the analytical lifecycle.

Publication governance requires:

- Citation information
- Methodological transparency
- Validation documentation
- Reproducibility records
- Archival preservation

Published outputs should remain independently verifiable.

---

8. Documentation Governance Principles

Documentation serves as the institutional memory of computational systems.

Documentation should include:

- Methodology
- Assumptions
- Provenance
- Validation
- Version history
- Citation information

Comprehensive documentation strengthens long-term sustainability.

---

9. Validation Governance Principles

Validation provides empirical support for analytical claims.

Validation procedures may include:

- Data validation
- Statistical validation
- Structural validation
- Sensitivity analysis
- Robustness assessment
- Reproducibility verification

Validation should remain transparent and independently reviewable.

---

10. Preservation Governance Principles

Long-term preservation protects analytical continuity.

Preservation mechanisms include:

- Repository archiving
- Metadata retention
- Manifest preservation
- DOI-linked publication
- Version-controlled storage

Preservation safeguards institutional memory and reproducibility.

---

11. Institutional Governance Framework

The FAIR+D Canon™ governance model operates across five institutional layers.

Layer| Governance Focus
Data Layer| Source integrity and provenance
Processing Layer| Computational accountability
Analytical Layer| Validation and methodological rigor
Publication Layer| Transparency and dissemination
Preservation Layer| Long-term reproducibility

These layers collectively establish a comprehensive governance architecture.

---

12. FAIR+D Compliance Requirements

Governance compliance is evaluated against the five FAIR+D dimensions.

Findable

Artifacts maintain persistent identifiers and discoverability mechanisms.

Accessible

Documentation and metadata remain available to authorized users.

Interoperable

Standardized structures support cross-platform integration.

Reusable

Artifacts preserve sufficient context for future application.

Defensible

Analytical claims remain transparent, auditable, and reproducible.
