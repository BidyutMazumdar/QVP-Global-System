## 1. What is QSSI 2026?

The Quantum Sovereign Stability Index (QSSI) 2026 is a multidimensional sovereign benchmarking framework designed to evaluate national institutional capacity across Artificial Intelligence (AI), Legal Governance (LEGAL), Post-Quantum Cybersecurity Preparedness (PQC), and National Resilience (RES).

The framework integrates multiple evidence domains into a unified, transparent, reproducible, and validation-oriented analytical architecture suitable for comparative international assessment.

---

## 2. What is the primary objective of QSSI?

The primary objective of QSSI is to provide a transparent, reproducible, statistically validated, and policy-relevant framework for evaluating sovereign institutional stability and preparedness in an era characterized by:

- Artificial Intelligence transformation
- Cybersecurity evolution
- Post-Quantum transition risks
- Governance complexity
- Digital statecraft
- Strategic resilience challenges
- Institutional modernization

---

## 3. How many countries are included?

QSSI 2026 evaluates:

**195 sovereign countries**

using a standardized global benchmarking framework.

---

## 4. What dimensions are measured?

QSSI integrates four principal dimensions:

### Artificial Intelligence Capacity (AI)

Measures national readiness for AI development, deployment, governance, adoption, innovation, and strategic capability.

### Legal Governance Capacity (LEGAL)

Measures governance quality, institutional effectiveness, regulatory capacity, accountability, and rule of law.

### Post-Quantum Cybersecurity Preparedness (PQC)

Measures readiness for emerging post-quantum cybersecurity and cryptographic transition requirements.

### National Resilience Capacity (RES)

Measures institutional resilience, preparedness capability, adaptive capacity, and strategic stability.

## 7. How are indicators normalized?

QSSI applies Min-Max normalization:

\[
\frac{x-\min(x)}{\max(x)-\min(x)}
\]

This procedure transforms indicators onto a common scale and ensures cross-domain comparability.

---

## 8. How is the final QSSI score calculated?

The framework combines three objective weighting systems:

### Entropy Weighting

Measures information content and indicator diversity.

### CRITIC Weighting

Measures contrast intensity and inter-indicator conflict.

### Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

Captures latent structural variance.

Hybrid weighting is calculated as:

\[
Hybrid=\frac{Entropy+CRITIC+PCA}{3}
\]

The final composite score is generated through weighted aggregation of normalized indicators.

---

## 9. Why are multiple weighting methods used?

Using multiple objective weighting systems:

- Reduces methodological bias
- Improves robustness
- Improves stability
- Minimizes dependence on a single weighting approach
- Enhances reproducibility
- Strengthens statistical validity

## 11. What is Monte Carlo Validation?

Monte Carlo validation repeatedly perturbs weighting structures and recomputes rankings.

Outputs include:

- Mean Rank
- Rank Standard Deviation
- Confidence Intervals
- Stability Scores
- Robustness Metrics

This process evaluates ranking reliability under uncertainty.

---

## 12. What is Sensitivity Analysis?

Sensitivity analysis evaluates how rankings respond to plausible weight perturbations.

Typical scenarios include:

- ±5%
- ±10%
- ±15%

weight variation testing.

---

## 13. What is Dominance Analysis?

Dominance analysis evaluates the relative contribution of each dimension to overall country performance and ranking outcomes.

---

## 14. What is Shapley Attribution?

Shapley attribution quantifies the marginal contribution of each framework component to the final composite score and supports transparent interpretability.

---

## 15. What are Confidence Scores?

Confidence Scores quantify data completeness.

```text
Confidence Score =
Observed Indicators / Total Indicators
```

Higher values indicate greater observational completeness.

---

## 33. What are the limitations of QSSI?

Like all composite benchmarking frameworks, QSSI has limitations.

These include:

- Dependence on underlying source datasets
- Potential measurement uncertainty
- Data availability constraints
- Temporal differences among source indicators
- Sensitivity to methodological assumptions
- Limitations inherent in cross-national comparisons

QSSI should therefore be interpreted as a structured benchmarking framework rather than a definitive measure of sovereign performance.

---

## 34. How should QSSI rankings be interpreted?

QSSI rankings should be interpreted as:

**Relative benchmarking outcomes**

rather than absolute measures of institutional quality.

Small score differences between neighboring countries may not necessarily imply substantial real-world differences in sovereign capability.

Users are encouraged to consider:

- Scores
- Confidence Tiers
- Validation Outputs
- Tier Classifications

alongside ranking positions.

## 37. Does QSSI evaluate military strength, GDP, or political ideology?

No.

QSSI is not designed to measure:

- Military power

- Electoral preferences

## 38. Is QSSI intended to compare governments or institutions?

QSSI primarily evaluates institutional capacity using internationally available indicators.

The framework should not be interpreted as an evaluation of individual political leaders, political parties, electoral outcomes, or specific administrations.
